Laroprovstat increased LDL uptake in HepG2 cells and increased liver Ldlr protein levels and lowered plasma LDL-C levels in human  PCSK9  knock-in mice. A , Concentration-response curve of laroprovstat effect on labeled low-density lipoprotein (LDL) uptake using proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)–treated HepG2 cells.  B , Liver LDL receptor (Ldlr) protein levels and ( C ) change in plasma LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from baseline in human  PCSK9  knock-in (hPCSK9 KI) mice dosed with vehicle or laroprovstat (30 mg·kg −1 ·d −1  for 7 days by oral gavage twice daily, n=11–12 mice per group).  D , Achieved (blue square) and predicted (solid line) laroprovstat plasma concentration. Data presented as individual values and mean±SEM. ** P <0.01, **** P <0.0001, unpaired t test. Laroprovstat increased uptake of labeled LDL in a concentration-dependent fashion in PCSK9-treated human hepatoma HepG2 cells with IC 50 125 nmol/L (95% CI, 80–324; Figure 3 A). In mice engineered to express human PCSK9 and not mouse Pcsk9 in hepatocytes, laroprovstat treatment increased mouse liver Ldlr protein levels and decreased plasma LDL-C levels (Figure 3 B and 3 C). Plasma exposures at termination were 2.01±1.30 μmol/L (Figure 3 D) and well predicted by the pharmacokinetic model ( Figure S3 ).
