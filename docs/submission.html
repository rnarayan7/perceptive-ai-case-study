Part I — Documentation

What was built

Two stages. Stage 1 reads numeric values off chart and table images and reports each with a method and a confidence. Stage 2 is the memo engine: it ingests public filings and literature, retrieves evidence, answers the four questions an investment in a drug developer resolves to, values the lead asset, and composes the memo. A FastAPI read layer and a Next.js app serve the result.

The memo answers each of the brief's four questions as an estimate, its evidence, and a confidence, then states where the view departs from the market:

  • Does the asset work (MoA) — mechanism synthesized from retrieved biology, every claim cited to a source chunk.
  • Will it be approved (PoS) — built from the scientific evidence the system retrieves for that asset, not a generic phase-transition rate.
  • How will it get approved (regulatory) — designations, pathway, and the next catalyst, from filings and FDA disclosure.
  • Commercial opportunity (peak sales) — five parameters, each labeled grounded or assumed, fed to a deterministic arithmetic step.

Work is split so the model never does what code can do reliably. The model retrieves, reads, and reasons. All arithmetic is in Python: the peak-sales estimate (estimate_peak_sales) and the rNPV revenue curve (valuation.py) are deterministic functions, because models drift over long arithmetic chains and the system does a lot of it.

In-house vs. off the shelf

Built here: a BM25 lexical retriever (stdlib, custom tokenizer that keeps drug codes intact), the deterministic peak-sales and rNPV math, the CV figure readers (pixel measurement of waterfall bars and Kaplan-Meier curves), the SQLite ledger that records every memo, claim, and run, the evidence-grounding harness, and the evaluation scorers.

Off the shelf: the Anthropic SDK for the reasoning model, click for the CLI, and urllib for ingestion. No vector database, no orchestration framework.

Where it is unreliable

The rNPV rests on a peak-sales estimate, and the estimate rests on five inputs. The system labels each one by how it was obtained. For KYMR, three are grounded in a source and two of the swing factors are assumptions:

Gross peak comes out near $41B. The population is real and the PoS is built from the asset's evidence, but penetration and net price are the two largest multipliers and both are assumptions. Flex either by a third and the rNPV moves by billions. So the honest reading of KYMR is not "the market is wrong by $3.5B"; it is "if a 15% share at $40k net holds, the asset screens cheap into the readout." The memo presents it that way rather than as a settled number.

The other soft spots

  • Single-asset valuation understates the pipeline. rNPV covers the lead asset only. A company with several shots on goal is worth more than its best one; the memo notes the rest carries little modeled value, which is a floor, not a fair value.
  • Figure reads carry method risk. On four hand-labeled real figures, CV pixel-measurement beat the vision model on waterfalls (it nails the extremes and the deep-response counts); the vision model was weaker there. Read-agreement between the two catches random error but not shared systematic bias.
  • The judge can flatter the generator. Faithfulness scoring uses a model to grade a model. It is meta-validated (below), but a judge sharing the generator's blind spots is a known limit, not a solved one.

What was omitted, and why

Gross-to-net pricing

Peak sales use a net price directly, with no rebate/discount bridge from list price.

Why: it is the single biggest structural lever and deserves grounded inputs, not a guessed haircut. The pricing data to do it (CMS ASP, Medicaid NADAC) is already ingested but not yet wired into the net-price parameter.

Per-claim figure annotation for all five

The figure corpus is ingested and shown; annotated insertion at each claim is not yet run across every company.

Why: synthetic stand-in figures were explicitly removed. Only real ingested images qualify, and wiring annotation to every claim is unfinished work, not a shortcut taken.

Sum-of-parts valuation

No multi-asset rollup; the memo values the lead program and flags the rest qualitatively.

Why: each additional asset needs its own grounded epi and PoS. Done badly it adds false precision; better to state the floor.

Consensus / analyst estimates

No programmatic pull of sell-side peak-sales or price targets.

Why: no free public source exists. The brief warns against reporting absence-of-enquiry as absence-of-data, so the memo derives its own number rather than implying a consensus it cannot see.

Dense retrieval

Retrieval is BM25 lexical only; the embedding reranker and RRF fusion exist but are off in v1.

Why: lexical retrieval already surfaces the right filings for these queries; the failures were selection inside a document, not recall. Dense was not the bottleneck.

Real-time price in the memo

Market cap and shares come from filings; live price is a cached daily snapshot, not fetched at compose time.

Why: the price module refuses to invent a number it cannot verify, and records it as a missing input instead.

When the system refuses

The system can decline to produce a memo. It refuses on structure, not on taste.

  • Ungrounded required section. Eight sections are required: thesis, overview, MoA, PoS, regulatory, peak sales, valuation, sources. If any of them ends with no evidence-backed claim, the memo is marked refused and names the failed section rather than fabricating one.
  • The one carve-out: peak sales may run on assumptions. It is forward-looking and has no programmatic commercial data source, so an all-assumption peak-sales section does not sink the memo — but every assumed input is labeled as such (as KYMR shows).
  • Model safety refusal. If the model returns a refusal stop-reason inside a module, that module degrades to empty rather than crashing the run, which can in turn cascade into a required-section refusal.
  • Data honesty, short of refusal. A failing source or a bad document is skipped, not fatal; unverifiable live figures (price, market cap, consensus) are recorded as missing inputs rather than invented.

External services and cost

One full memo run costs ~$0.40 on claude-sonnet-5 (the five-company batch ran $0.37 to $0.45, ~85k input / ~22k output tokens, roughly four minutes each). Cost is recorded per run in the ledger's generation_runs table, not estimated.

What a further month would fix

In priority order, by leverage on the numbers:

  • Wire net price to ingested pricing. CMS ASP and NADAC are already in the corpus. Grounding annual net price to them, with a real gross-to-net bridge, removes the single largest assumption and would most change KYMR's rNPV.
  • Ground peak penetration to analogs. Derive a share range from comparable launches in the same indication rather than a flat 15%.
  • Per-claim figure annotation across all five. Insert and mark the real ingested figures at the claim each supports, which is the part of the figures brief still outstanding.
  • Sum-of-parts. Extend rNPV to the next one or two assets so the valuation is a range, not a lead-asset floor.
  • Turn on dense retrieval fusion for the cases where the right value sits in prose the lexical index ranks low.

Part II — Evaluation framework

How you would demonstrate it works

The point of this section is not to assert the system works. It is to describe the measurements that would show it, and which of them run today. The design principle throughout: numeric scoring is deterministic; a model is used only where the judgment is genuinely linguistic, and never on the numeric path.

deterministic no model, reproducible model needs a key, judge-based
EvaluationWhat it measuresType
retrievalhit@k, recall@k, precision@k, MRR against gold queriesdet.
epi-retrievaldo the peak-sales queries surface prevalence for the lead indicationdet.
epi-groundingis the epidemiology population cited to an epi source and in a plausible rangegen. model, det. score
faithfulnesseach claim scored supported / partial / unsupported / contradicted vs. its cited evidencejudge
memogrades the latest ledger memo: faithfulness plus a 1-5 quality rubric per modulejudge
figure evalwithin-tolerance / MAE, calibration (ECE), read-agreement vs. error, error categoriesdet. score

The figure harness reports more than accuracy. Calibration (ECE) asks whether stated confidence is honest. The keystone metric asks whether read-disagreement between two independent methods predicts error, since that is the signal you would triage on in production. It only catches random error by design; the report says so.

Verification that is genuine

Models affirm what they are shown, so "one component checks another" is only meaningful if the check is substantive. Four places where it is:

  • Two independent methods, not two samples. On figures, the CV reader measures pixels and the vision model reads the image. When they agree it is a real cross-check between different methods; when they disagree it flags a figure to triage. Sampling one model twice cannot do this.
  • The judge is validated before its scores are trusted. Known-answer probes check the judge's exact-verdict accuracy. A known-bad baseline feeds it an evidence-shuffled memo and requires the faithfulness score to collapse by a margin; if a corrupted memo scores as well as a real one, the judge is measuring nothing.
  • Judge against human. A calibration set scores judge-vs-human agreement with Cohen's kappa, so the judge's reliability is a measured number, not an assumption.
  • Citations cannot be fabricated. The grounding harness lets the model cite only labeled evidence IDs, which are resolved back to real source chunks. A claim either resolves to a real chunk or it does not exist.
The honest limit

Faithfulness still uses a model to grade a model, and a judge can share the generator's blind spots. The meta-validation above bounds that risk; it does not erase it. The strongest signal in the system is where two different methods must agree, which is why the figure cross-check matters more than any single judge score.
